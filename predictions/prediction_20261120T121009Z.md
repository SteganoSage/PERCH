# Pre-registered predictions: live chaos campaign

Written 2026-11-20 12:10 UTC and committed **before** the campaign it predicts. Graded on
reasoning, not accuracy: where a prediction misses, the follow-up is a mechanistic explanation,
not a revised number.

## What is being predicted

The live campaign `analysis/run_campaign.py --repeats 5`: every scenario under every decision mode,
5 runs per cell, 75 runs. Stack defaults: 50 rps open-loop load (`work:2, work/checkout:1`), 1%
background errors on both versions, ramp 5 → 10 → 25 → 50 → 100% with a 25s bake, controller poll
every 5s, Prometheus scrape every 2s. The runner starts the load generator, waits until Prometheus
shows at least 250 stable requests in the window, then starts the controller.

| Scenario | Canary fault (on top of the 1% background) |
|---|---|
| good | none |
| bad-error | 30% of requests fail (≈31% total) |
| bad-latency | ~400ms ± 120ms instead of ~40ms ± 10ms |
| bad-slow-onset | errors ramp linearly 0 → 30% over 120s from canary start |
| bad-partial | 60% of `/work/checkout` fails; that's a third of the mix (≈21% overall) |

Metrics, per run (from `summary.json`): outcome; **time to outcome** (seconds from the controller's
first decision to its rollback/promote decision); **canary requests** (requests the canary served,
from the load generator's log: the blast radius); canary errors served.

## Method

**Start from the simulator.** `analysis/simulate_rollouts.py` drives the controller's real decision
and ramp code with synthetic traffic; `results/experiments/simulation/e1_modes.csv` has 1,000 runs
per cell at exactly the campaign's settings. It already reproduced the disclosed live runs (tier1
bad-error: 20.0s and 50 requests simulated vs 20.0s and 48/51 live; good rollouts: 125–135s
simulated vs 133–135s live).

**Then adjust for four things the simulator leaves out:**

1. **Reload lag.** In the simulator a rollback takes effect at the decision. Live, nginx picks up the
   new config within ~1s (the proxy polls the file once a second), so the canary keeps serving for
   up to a second: about +1 to +3 requests at the 5% stage (2.5 canary requests/s).
2. **Scrape staleness.** The controller sees counters as of Prometheus's last scrape, up to 2s old.
   At the first poll after the controller starts, the canary has had ~5s of traffic, minus up to
   ~1s before nginx reloads to 5%, minus up to 2s of staleness: the controller sees ~6–10 canary
   requests, not the simulator's 12–13. Detectors that act at the first poll (naive, tier2 on loud
   faults) will sometimes need a second poll live where the simulator needed one.
3. **Poll drift.** Live polls average 5.32–5.39s, not 5.0s (occasional 7.7s ticks in all three
   archived good runs). Times stretch ~6%, and each poll carries a few more requests.
4. **In-flight requests.** A slow request is only counted when it finishes: under bad-latency each
   canary request reaches Prometheus ~0.4s late, which matters only for a detector waiting on a
   sample count (tier1).

Mechanisms 1 and 2 cost about one poll for first-poll detectors and almost nothing for tier1,
whose 40-request floor (four polls) dominates either way.

Reasoning for the cells that differ most from the simulator:

- **tier2 bad-error (22 vs sim 17.5).** Its rollback boundary is an LLR of 5.19 (alpha split over
  10 tests). Against stable's ~1%, each error adds ~1.77 and each success subtracts ~0.05, so it
  needs about 4 errors in the first ~10 requests. With ~6–10 requests visible at the first poll
  (mechanism 2), that happens in roughly a quarter to a third of runs; the rest roll back at the
  second poll (~10s, ~25 requests served). The simulator, seeing 12 at the first poll, rolls back
  there about half the time.
- **naive bad-error stays at the first poll.** It needs just one error among the ~6–10 visible
  requests (P ≈ 0.9–0.97 at 31%), so the staleness costs it only the reload lag.
- **tier1 bad-latency (55 vs 50).** Its latency signal waits for 40 *completed* canary requests;
  at 20s about 41–45 have been both completed and scraped, close enough to the floor that some
  runs need a fifth poll (25s, ~62 requests).
- **bad-latency for naive and tier2 (14).** Two slow requests already push tier2's latency LLR past
  the boundary (each adds ~4.0), and naive's p95 rule fires on any visible slow request, so both
  act at the first poll and pay only the reload lag.
- **bad-slow-onset** keeps the simulator's ordering: tier2 slowest, because each stage's test may
  accept "healthy" while the fault is still small and only the next stage's fresh test catches it;
  naive most variable, because it fires on the first unlucky poll as the rate climbs.

## Falsifiable claims

Each is checked against the campaign's `table.md`/`runs.jsonl` and marked hit or miss.

- **P1. No bad canary escapes.** All 60 bad-scenario runs roll back in every mode; none is
  promoted. (Simulator: 100% in 1,000 runs per cell. A single promotion is a miss.)
- **P2. Loud-fault ordering.** On bad-error, bad-latency and bad-partial, mean canary requests
  rank naive ≤ tier2 < tier1 in each scenario.
- **P3. tier1's floor.** tier1's canary requests on bad-error and bad-partial have sd < 5 and mean
  in 47–54: the 40-request floor, not the data, sets its blast radius.
- **P4. The live tax.** For naive and tier2 on bad-error and bad-partial, the live mean exceeds the
  simulator's by 1–8 requests; for tier1 on the same scenarios the live mean is within ±4 of 50.
  (A live mean *below* the simulator's for naive/tier2 would mean the staleness reasoning is wrong.)
- **P5. Slow onset is tier2's worst case.** tier2 has the highest mean canary requests on
  bad-slow-onset; naive has the largest sd there.
- **P6. False rollbacks of good canaries.** The simulator's rates are 12.2% (naive), 7.6% (tier1)
  and 1.3% (tier2) per run. With 5 runs each: P(at least one) ≈ 48%, 33% and 6%. Prediction:
  tier2 promotes 5/5; naive and tier1 together show 0–2 false rollbacks in 10 runs (expected 1.0,
  P(none) ≈ 35%). Five runs cannot *measure* these rates; the simulator carries that claim. Live,
  this is only a gross check: 2 or more tier2 false rollbacks in 5 (P ≈ 0.2% under 1.3%) would mean
  the simulator misses something about live noise.
- **P7. Rollout time.** Every promoted good run takes 128–150s, longer than the simulator's 125s
  floor because of poll drift.
- **P8. Errors served.** On bad-error, mean canary errors served rank naive < tier2 < tier1, with
  tier1 about 3× tier2.

## What would surprise me, and what it would mean

- **tier2 slower than tier1 on a loud fault.** The sequential test would be losing evidence between
  polls (a counter or stage-reset bug) rather than waiting for it.
- **A false rollback in a bad-latency run attributed to error_rate** (or the reverse). The signal
  fusion would be misattributing evidence.
- **Live times well under the simulator's.** The poll-drift reading of the archived runs would be
  wrong.
- **naive false-rolling-back more than 2 of 5 good canaries.** Live noise would be burstier than
  independent per-request failures (e.g. errors clustering in time), which would also be a reason
  to distrust every simulated false-rollback rate.
